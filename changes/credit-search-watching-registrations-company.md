news: Four new tools. Credit search asks Google for pages that print a client's photo credit and checks their pictures. Watching reads only the new posts of sites and accounts with 2 or more Stolen copies, once a week. Clients › Registrations lists photos with copies but no VA number yet. Who's behind this site now gathers every name the site goes by (Terms of service too), follows the firm's research steps (names, brand to company, is it active, Delaware last) and keeps a company card with the firm's own fields and status: Seemingly viable, Not a viable entity or Not checked yet.
timeline: Credit search, Watching, Registrations, company card
feature 4: **Credit search.** Once a week per wording, in your hours, PageProof asks Google for pages that print a client's photo credit ("Photo: Harbor Times", made-up example) and compares their pictures with that client's photos. Matches go to New as "Found by credit search"; pages that credit the client but show none of their photos are listed to look at by hand. A robot check rests Google for an hour and is never got around.
feature 4: **Watching.** A site or social account with 2 or more Stolen copies is watched: once a week only its new posts (its news feed or sitemap, or the account's newest posts) are compared with every client's photos. Old Stolen pages are never opened again. Add or stop watching any site or account by hand.
feature 5: **Registrations.** Clients › Registrations lists photos that have copies found but only an application number or no number, Stolen ones first, with a list to copy for the registrar. A VA number added later moves the photo to Registered lately.
feature 5: **Find the company, the firm's way.** Who's behind this site lists every name the site goes by (footer, Terms of service, Privacy, About, the site's data, the domain record and the site's Facebook Page transparency), marks each as a brand or a company, and gives a 3-step checklist: names, brand to company (Justia, USPTO, OpenCorporates, SEC), is it active (state search, past lawsuits, DMCA agent directory, Delaware last).
feature 5: **Company card and status.** Each site gets a company card with the firm's fields (entity name type, entity name, other names, state, entity number, what the registry says, registered agent, addresses, DMCA agent) and status: Seemingly viable, Not a viable entity or Not checked yet. The status shows on every copy from that site, Ready for legal needs an active company, and copies on a site that is not a viable entity go to the bottom.
